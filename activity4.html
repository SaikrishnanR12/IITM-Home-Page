03 Formation

Why Binomial
📋 TACTICS BOARD

Each match is one independent trial, two outcomes — home win (success) or not. With n fixed trials per batch and p close to 0.5, the shape comes out close to symmetric — unlike the earlier goals-per-match model (Poisson, λ = 1.355), which is right-skewed since it counts unbounded rare events. A Binomial with p near 0.5 has no such constraint, so it centers and balances.

05 Final Whistle

Comparing the Fits

A chi-square goodness-of-fit statistic — Σ(observed − expected)² / expected, using batch counts — was computed for each model directly in the spreadsheet.

📋 THE FORMULA
χ² GOODNESS-OF-FIT
χ² = Σx (Ox − Ex)² / Ex
O = observed batch count at x, E = batches × Binomial P(X=x). Raw totals aren't directly comparable across models with different category counts, so each is also divided by its degrees of freedom ("reduced χ²") to put both on the same scale.

🏆 Both reduced χ² values sit comfortably below 1 — a rough sign neither model is a poor fit relative to its own category count. On this normalized measure, n = 20 is, if anything, marginally closer to its theoretical curve (0.77 vs 0.87). That backs n = 10 as a natural, reasonable choice of batch size (it fits well and mirrors the earlier goals-per-match analysis in structure), while showing the model isn't fragile to that choice — n = 20 fits at least as well.
