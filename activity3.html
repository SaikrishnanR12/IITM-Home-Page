📊 Central tendency & dispersion — numerical variables 🧮

These are the exact "Plot of Variable_1" – "_5" histograms from the spreadsheet — same IQR-fence bins, same counts. Mean, median, mode, range, std dev, and IQR are the live-computed numbers listed under each chart (not separately charted in the spreadsheet either).

🧮 Formulas used
Mean (x̄) x̄ = Σxᵢn Sum of all values divided by the count n.
Median x₍₍ₙ₊₁₎∕₂₎ if n is odd  ·  x₍ₙ∕₂₎ + x₍ₙ∕₂ ₊ ₁₎2 if n is even The middle value once the data is sorted (average of the two middle values for an even n).
Mode Mode = xᵢ where f(xᵢ) = max(f) The value that occurs with the highest frequency.
Range R = max(x) − min(x) Distance between the largest and smallest observation.
Variance (s²) s² = Σ(xᵢ − x̄)²n − 1 Average squared deviation from the mean (sample formula, dividing by n − 1).
Std Dev (s) s = √Σ(xᵢ − x̄)²n − 1 Square root of the variance — deviation expressed back in the variable's own units.
IQR IQR = Q₃ − Q₁ Spread of the middle 50% of the data — the 75th percentile minus the 25th.
🗂️ Central tendency — categorical variables 🛰️

Mirrors Plot of Variable_6–_8 — top categories by count, with the mode 🏆 highlighted in amber, plus the Index of Qualitative Variation for each.

🧮 Formulas used
Mode Mode = category cᵢ where f(cᵢ) = max(f) Only measure of central tendency that's valid for nominal/categorical data — mean and median don't apply.
IQV IQV = k(N² − Σfᵢ²)N²(k − 1) Index of Qualitative Variation — k = number of categories, N = total count, fᵢ = frequency of category i. Ranges 0 (no variation, one category dominates) to 1 (perfectly even spread).

🔗 Covariance & correlation ✨
🧮 Formulas used
Covariance Cov(X,Y) = Σ(xᵢ − x̄)(yᵢ − ȳ)n − 1 Average of the products of each variable's deviation from its own mean — positive when X and Y tend to move together.
Correlation (r) r = Cov(X,Y)sₓ · sᵧ = Σ(xᵢ − x̄)(yᵢ − ȳ)√[Σ(xᵢ − x̄)² · Σ(yᵢ − ȳ)²] Covariance rescaled by both standard deviations so the result is unit-free and bounded between −1 and 1 — this is the Pearson correlation coefficient.

Pearson correlation — moderately strong 💪
🧠 Perigee and apogee move together — the positive covariance confirms it. But covariance's units (km²) make it hard to read on its own, so correlation does the real talking. r ≈ 0.62 says moderately strong and positive: makes sense, since the two together define an orbit's shape, and near-circular orbits naturally pull them close. It's not closer to 1 because a chunk of satellites live in stretched-out elliptical orbits, scattering the tight near-circular cluster. 🛰️💫
